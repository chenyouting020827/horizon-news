## [1960 'Doomsday' Paper's 2026 Date Resurfaces Online](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.132.3436.1291) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A 1960 Science paper by Heinz von Foerster, Patricia M. Mora, and Lawrence W. Amiot, which extrapolated hyperbolic human population growth to a literal 'doomsday' on Friday, 13 November 2026, resurfaced and trended on Hacker News as that date approaches. It is a vivid case study in why naive trend extrapolation fails, and the discussion uses it to scrutinize contemporary forecasts such as AI-risk scenarios, orbital data centers, and demographic decline. The prediction depends on fitting historical population data to a hyperbolic curve that diverges to infinity at a finite date; in reality global population growth has slowed, and many industrialized countries now worry more about decline than overpopulation.

hackernews · voxleone · Nov 13, 12:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50482379)

**Background**: Hyperbolic growth is a mathematical pattern in which the growth rate rises with population size, producing a curve that becomes vertical at a finite time. Heinz von Foerster and colleagues observed that world population had followed such a pattern for millennia and calculated a singularity on 13 November 2026, which they labeled 'Doomsday.' The paper became a famous cautionary tale about demographic forecasting and extrapolation, even though population growth later slowed rather than diverging to infinity.

## [Developer turns 1,300 sq mi of SF Bay Area 3D tiles into AI pixel-art map](https://isometricsf.com/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Developer Justin Chavez released Isometric SF, an explorable isometric pixel-style map covering roughly 1,300 square miles of the San Francisco Bay Area, built by running Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles through custom LoRA models. He first prototyped the style with Nano Banana 2 to train a Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 LoRA, then trained a smaller Klein 4B LoRA that reproduced the look at about one quarter of the cost. The project shows how far a single developer can push a generative pipeline over real geospatial data, turning Google's photorealistic 3D tiles into a stylized, navigable artifact at a fraction of frontier-model cost. It also adds a concrete data point to the ongoing debate over whether AI-generated imagery can legitimately be called pixel art, a question likely to keep recurring as style-transfer LoRAs spread. Chavez estimates that generating the whole map directly with Nano Banana 2 would have cost around $7,000, which is why the cheaper Klein 4B LoRA was used for the final output. Commenters pointed out that the underlying imagery is Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles API, which offers about 1,000 free API calls per month, and that fine details are imperfect — buildings can be blended from two colors where the source is uniform.

hackernews · juscha1 · Nov 13, 17:58 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487228)

**Background**: Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles are a Maps Tile API product that streams high-resolution 3D mesh and texture data for thousands of cities, letting developers build immersive 3D visualizations. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is a lightweight fine-tuning technique that adapts a large image model to reproduce a specific subject or style without retraining the whole model, producing small shareable weight files. Qwen-Image-Edit is Alibaba's roughly 20B-parameter image editing model, and the 2511 / Klein variants referenced here are its later releases and smaller distilled counterparts. The project was inspired by Isometric NYC, an earlier pixel-art city map.

A security write-up titled "Ctrl+C, Ctrl+v, Ctrl+cve" demonstrates methods for bypassing Android's clipboard read restrictions, according to the post at toninoble.com. It focuses on how apps may still obtain clipboard data despite the OS-level privacy measures introduced in recent Android versions. Clipboard data frequently contains passwords, one-time codes, addresses, and other sensitive text, so a bypass of Android's read restrictions could undermine a key privacy safeguard for billions of mobile users. The research adds pressure on Google and OEMs to harden clipboard APIs and on app developers to adopt user-initiated paste flows. Android 10 introduced the core clipboard privacy change: background apps can no longer silently read the clipboard, while foreground apps and the default input method still need clipboard access to support copy-and-paste. The write-up's title is a pun on CVE, the standard identifier for publicly disclosed security vulnerabilities, but the provided summary does not name a specific CVE or detail the exact exploit chain.

hackernews · dethmasque · Nov 13, 18:23 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487605)

**Background**: Historically, Android's clipboard framework allowed essentially any app to read from or write to the clipboard, which enabled third-party clipboard managers but also let apps snoop on copied text. Android 10 tightened this by restricting background clipboard access and notifying users when an app reads the clipboard, though foreground apps can still access it. GrapheneOS has since added a "secure paste" feature that aims to expose clipboard contents only through a user-initiated paste action.

Gödel is one of the most original thinkers of the 20th century, and his unpublished work runs to tens of thousands of pages, so surfacing proved theorems could reshape how historians and logicians understand his later thought. rss · The Guardian World · Nov 13, 16:38

## [Live GPU-Accelerated SDR Feed Captures the Whole Airband Around Orcas Island](https://orcasairband.com/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A hobbyist operator (mmaunder) launched orcasairband.com, a live receiver built on an Ettus USRP B210 attached to a dual RTX 4090 machine, which uses a CUDA-based polyphase channelizer to split the entire VHF aviation airband into channels, demodulate them, detect transmissions and transcribe them in real time. The feed happened to go live on a day when a ground stop hit Victoria and Vancouver, so it captured genuine pilot and controller chatter about the situation, including a 14:03 exchange confirming "there's a ground stop going into Vancouver on this frequency." It shows that a couple of commodity GPUs plus a mid-range SDR can turn an entire swath of spectrum into a continuously transcribed, searchable feed — a capability that once required dedicated signal-intelligence hardware. That lowers the barrier for radio hobbyists, aviation enthusiasts and researchers, while also raising familiar questions about how easily public analog voice channels can now be monitored and mined at scale. The transcripts are raw, unedited automatic speech recognition output, so many transmissions come through garbled or marked "unintelligible," and weak distant signals such as Victoria Ground (126.400) degrade badly. The system discards non-transmission data to keep the pipeline manageable, and the whole thing runs on a wideband capture of roughly 108–137 MHz, with voice communications occupying 118.0–136.975 MHz.

hackernews · mmaunder · Nov 13, 22:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50490593)

**Background**: Software-defined radio (SDR) replaces analog mixers, filters and demodulators with software running on a general-purpose computer, so one piece of hardware can receive many different protocols. The VHF airband used for aviation voice traffic sits between 108 and 137 MHz, with the lowest 10 MHz reserved for navigation aids and the rest divided into narrow channels at 25 kHz spacing in the US. A channelizer is a digital signal-processing block that takes one wideband digitized signal and splits it into many narrowband outputs at once; a polyphase filter bank is the efficient standard way to do this, and it maps naturally onto GPU parallelism. That lets a single wideband capture replace hundreds of separate analog receiver chains.
